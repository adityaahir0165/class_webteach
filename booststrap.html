Pablo Escobar was a notorious Colombian drug lord who co-founded and led the infamous Medellín Cartel during the late 20th century. Born in 1949, he rose from humble beginnings to become one of the wealthiest and most powerful criminals in history, monopolizing the global cocaine trade and supplying an estimated 80% of the drug smuggled into the United States at the height of his power. Often referred to as the "King of Cocaine," Escobar's immense wealth allowed him to cultivate a dual persona; while he terrorized Colombia with ruthless violence, bombings, and assassinations of politicians and journalists, he also styled himself as a Robin Hood figure by building housing, schools, and soccer fields for the poor in Medellín. His reign of narco-terrorism ultimately ended on December 2, 1993, when he was shot and killed by the Colombian National Police on a Medellín rooftop while attempting to flee.
